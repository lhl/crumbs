# Demo video tooling: food grid, globe capture, slides

## Summary

Tools to assemble the demo video from the real app and the local corpus. All inputs (shot config with photo ids and labels, voiceover) and outputs (frames, slides, mp4) live in the git-ignored `demo/video/` directory; the scripts contain no personal data.

- **`demo/tools/render_intro.py`:** a 64x36 grid of photos whose latest classification is food (2,304 of 2,530, evenly sampled in time order, from the local 1024 px analysis images). Tiles pop in at random, the grid zooms in slowly, then at a cue time it swipes up into the globe frames. It writes the video only, plus a preview with the voiceover muxed in.
- **`demo/tools/capture_globe.py`:** drives the real PWA (`?demo=1`, local tiles, heatmap) frame by frame in headless Chromium. Each frame:
  - sets the scripted camera;
  - positions food callouts (photo preview from `crumbs serve`, dish label, city and month) at each photo's real map position, using `map.project`;
  - types the Ask question in sync with the voiceover and presses Ask.

  Frame-by-frame capture keeps the motion smooth with software WebGL: about 0.65 s per frame in isolation.
- **`demo/tools/make_slides.py`:** a model slide (license, model, parameters, size, precision, with in-pipeline vs tested chips) and an accuracy-and-speed slide (food F1 table plus a seconds-per-photo table side by side, and the no-training decision-model note), with numbers taken from MODELS.md.

Content choices (lead's privacy rules):
- The callout photos were reviewed visually. One candidate showing a person was replaced, as were a menu-only shot and a dark shot.
- The camera stays at globe zoom, where nothing finer than city level is visible.
- The grid uses 30 px tiles; people in food photos are not identifiable at that size, but the lead should still review the final frames.
- PE-Core precision was never recorded in our runs, so the slide says "not recorded".

## Validation

- `python3 -m py_compile demo/tools/*.py`.
- A 1 fps test capture showed callout dots aligned with Taipei, Okinawa, Kyoto, and Tokyo on the heatmap.
- A 6 s test render showed pop-in, the full grid, and the swipe into the globe; 7.1 s of video took 21.7 s to render.
- Both slides were rendered at 1920x1080 and checked visually (no wrapping after the column fix).
- The full 30 fps globe capture (597 frames) was in progress at the time of this entry.
