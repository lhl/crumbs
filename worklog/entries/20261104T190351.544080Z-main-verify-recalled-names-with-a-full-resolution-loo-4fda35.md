# Verify recalled names with a full-resolution look

## Summary

The demo's anchored question (worklog 20261104T172300) reported a wrong restaurant name. The name came from a stored extraction of a chopstick sleeve that misread one character. The lead identified the real restaurant by matching the photos' GPS and a web lookup of the corrected name against a public listing.

Fix:
- The recall skill now checks every name before reporting it. Each `day_timeline` moment lists `text_photo_ids` (photos with distinct text, at most 3), and Glimmer calls `look_at_photos` on them.
- `look_at_photos` now reads the 1024 px analysis image instead of the 640 px preview. On that photo the preview misread the name and the analysis image read it correctly.
- After a look, the next turn is answer-only, with a nudge to lead with the closer reading.
- Agent output budget raised from 700 to 4096 tokens. Glimmer reasons even with `reasoning_effort: none`, that reasoning counts against `max_tokens`, and two answer turns came back empty or cut off mid-sentence. Prompts stay near 3k tokens, so 4096 fits the 8k context, and no separate reasoning cap is needed.

## Changes

- `crumbs/moments.py`: moments carry `text_photos`, one per distinct reading; `moment_line` exposes `text_photo_ids`.
- `crumbs/agent.py`:
  - text photos are citable and lookable, and the vision check runs on the photo itself;
  - `_vision` uses the analysis image, falling back to the preview;
  - forced answer turn after a look, with the nudge;
  - `MAX_LOOKS` 3 and `MAX_TOKENS` 4096.
- `crumbs/skills/recall.md`: step d now checks the name with a closer look before reporting it.
- `tests/test_agent.py`: timeline offers the sign photo; the look reads the sign photo itself; the turn after a look offers no tools.
- `MODELS.md`: look path, measured effect, latency and token budget.

## Validation

- `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests`: 37 passed.
- Live on the zbook Q4_K_M server, on the real store (values kept out of the repo):
  - The closer look read the correct name on every run.
  - Before the forced answer turn and the larger budget: of 3 runs, 1 led with the stored misread, 1 ended in a 500 after a junk extra call, and 1 was correct.
  - With the 700-token budget plus the forced turn: of 3 runs, 1 empty answer, 1 cut off mid-sentence, and 1 that did not lead with the name. A token-usage probe showed answer turns with about 465 completion tokens, most of them reasoning.
  - Final configuration: 3/3 runs led with the correct reading, 68.3 s, 80.0 s and 71.2 s, 0 unsupported citations.
- PWA (headless Chromium, read-only query): same answer, trace "3 lookups · 77.9s", 1 card, 4 thumbnails, 0 console errors.
